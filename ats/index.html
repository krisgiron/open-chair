The basics Myths, retired What you do What changed Cheat sheet Sources and upkeep What an ATS actually is A filing cabinet with a search bar. If you have applied for a job in the last few years, an ATS has touched your resume. If you have gotten a rejection email at 2 AM and wondered what kind of robot judged your career while you slept, this guide is for you. The short version: the ATS is not the villain of your job search. But not understanding it might be. ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Ashby, BambooHR, they are all versions of the same thing. It collects applications, pulls the text of your resume into database fields, and gives the hiring team one place to post jobs, track candidates through stages, schedule interviews, and send emails. That is the whole job. One recruiter put it best: the T stands for Tracking, not Terminator. How common is it, really In 2026, Jobscan detected an applicant tracking system on the career pages of 97.4% of Fortune 500 companies, which is 487 out of 500. Verified Sep 2026 The other 13 most likely built something in house. That number has barely moved in eight years, so treat "big company means an ATS" as a safe assumption. Which ones you will actually meet Inside the Fortune 500, the field is concentrated. Workday alone is used by more than 40% of the list, and Workday plus SuccessFactors together account for 52.4%. Verified Sep 2026 Outside the Fortune 500 it spreads out considerably. Across more than 13,000 companies where Jobscan detected a system, Greenhouse came in at 19.0%, Workday at 17.5%, Lever at 15.6%, and iCIMS at 14.4%. Verified Sep 2026 Practical takeaway: if you are applying to startups and mid-size companies, you will see Greenhouse and Lever far more often than the enterprise names. The journey of one application Here is what actually happens between clicking Submit and hearing back. 1 You apply Resume uploaded, questions answered. 2 Knockout questions "3+ years experience?" "Authorized to work?" This is the only true auto rejection point in the building. A human wrote these rules. This is where the 2 AM rejection email comes from. 3 Your resume is parsed Text pulled into database fields: jobs, dates, titles, skills. The parsed version, not your beautiful PDF, is what the system stores and searches. 4 You wait in the database With everyone else. 5 A recruiter searches Keywords, filters, ranked lists. You are either found here or you are invisible here. This is the step worth optimizing. 6 A human skims Seconds, not minutes. The top third of page one wins. 7 A human decides Advance, hold, or pass. A person, every time. Referrals enter at this step and skip the whole queue. Steps 2 and 3 are software. Steps 5, 6, and 7 are people. Almost nobody's resume is rejected by a robot for missing a keyword. It just never gets found in step 5. That is the problem worth solving. From the other side of the desk I have run Ashby from the admin side, and the hard part was never a lack of qualified people. It was volume. I remember sitting down to a stack of 68 resumes and feeling my attention shrink by the tenth one, my scans getting shorter and shorter, knowing I would never get through them all. So give yourself some grace. When you do not hear back, it is usually not a verdict on you. It is a tired human buried in volume.
